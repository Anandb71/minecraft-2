## Design Context

### Users
Everyone who lands on the page: players and fans deciding whether to try it,
developers and recruiters curious how it is built, and people who were sent
a screenshot. They arrive from a link on a phone or a laptop and decide in
seconds whether it is worth their time; the job is "show me what this is,
then tell me how to get it running", with the engineering there for whoever
scrolls further.

### Brand Personality
Epic, earthy, hand-built. A big-budget game's confidence with Minecraft's
warmth: the imagery does the shouting, the words stay plain and friendly,
and the numbers behind it are real. It should make someone ask "one person
made this?"

### Aesthetic Direction
A cinematic trailer site. Full-bleed, colour-graded screenshots from the
real engine carry every chapter, with slow, scroll-tied motion; huge
condensed poster type over the imagery (in the manner of a Rockstar launch
site), dark and moody, minimal chrome. Warmth comes from a sunlit amber
accent, earthy tinted neutrals and small blocky, pixel-scale details (in
the manner of Minecraft.net). Dark theme: this is watched like a trailer.
References: Rockstar's GTA VI site, Minecraft.net. Anti-references: purple
or cyan gradients, glowing glass cards, identical icon feature grids, hero
metric templates.

### Design Principles
1. The engine's own pictures are the design: nothing competes with them.
2. One idea a chapter, told in a title, two lines and a picture.
3. Motion is slow, tied to scroll, and optional (reduced motion gets a still,
   equally good page).
4. Every number is true and comes from the repo.
5. Getting it running is never more than one click away.
